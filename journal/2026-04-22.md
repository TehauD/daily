# The Daily: Adoption Needs a Control Plane

AI adoption slows down when every promising use case has to rediscover the same path through access, security, testing, and review. The friction is not only in the model. It is in the operating system around the model.

Today I presented an AI adoption approach and advanced the next steps for a contract evaluation agent. The discussion made the governance layers visible: a non-production environment, an approved connector policy, representative test material, and domain review of both inputs and outputs.

## The theme: Scale the path, not just the prototype

A successful demonstration can prove that a task is possible. It does not prove the solution is ready to be trusted, supported, or repeated.

The contract agent needed two parallel workstreams. The technical workstream required a governed environment and the connector configuration needed for standard functionality. The evaluation workstream required domain reviewers to curate examples and validate whether the agent's findings were accurate.

Neither workstream can substitute for the other. A secure environment does not make the analysis correct. A good answer in a demo does not make the deployment governed.

The adoption program also had a capacity problem. More people were interested in co-leading than there were prepared projects ready for them. That is a useful signal. The limiting factor was not enthusiasm. It was the ability to source, shape, and govern enough real work.

## Field notes from the build

I framed adoption as a layered model rather than a collection of isolated AI experiments. A project needs a business problem, an accountable owner, a supported platform path, and an evaluation plan. Without those pieces, a prototype can become an orphaned artifact.

The legal use case was a concrete example. The immediate actions were to follow up on approved non-production environments, obtain final approval for the required DLP connectors, and coordinate domain review of the agent's input and output accuracy. Those actions were documented, but they were not yet complete.

The strongest design choice was putting domain validation directly into the plan. Contract analysis contains judgment that cannot be reduced to a technical smoke test. Reviewers need to examine whether the reference material is appropriate, whether the output identifies meaningful variances, and whether the presentation supports human decision-making.

The discussion also called for additional workflows that could benefit from co-leadership and AI adoption. I am treating that as a sourcing challenge, not evidence of new projects delivered. A healthy portfolio should not reward the number of ideas collected. It should reward the number of well-shaped opportunities that can enter a controlled delivery path. #type/win

Calendar evidence alone was not enough for the rest of the day. Two other sessions were canceled, and a tentative one-to-one did not establish attendance or completed work. I excluded them from the accomplishments. #type/win

-

-

-

Identified project sourcing and shaping as a constraint on scaling the adoption program.

## The reusable pattern

Create a standard entry path for AI work.

Each use case should declare the problem, owner, data boundary, platform, required connectors, human review point, evaluation set, success criteria, and promotion gate. Provisioning and policy checks should be reusable so project teams spend their time on the domain problem instead of reconstructing governance.

The future state is a configuration-driven control plane. An approved intake record could generate a non-production workspace, assign the right groups, apply the policy baseline, create an evaluation template, and open the required review tasks.

## Standing takeaways

-

A demonstration proves possibility, not readiness.

-

Security approval and accuracy evaluation are separate release gates.

-

Domain experts should validate both the test inputs and the agent outputs.

-

A shortage of prepared projects can constrain adoption more than a shortage of volunteers.

-

Canceled invitations are not evidence of work completed. #type/win

## Open thread

The next step is to complete the environment and connector prerequisites, then run a curated evaluation with explicit expected findings and reviewer feedback.
