# The Daily: Environment Sprawl Is a Design Smell

Isolation is useful until every request creates another environment, policy, group, and support obligation. Today I worked through a Power Platform baseline and found the more durable boundary: group solutions around stable ownership, then isolate only the exceptions that justify it.

The discussion covered delayed environment setup, security-group administration, naming conventions, DLP assignment, support sandboxes, and connector management. The strongest outcome was a clearer direction away from agent-specific environments and toward team-focused governance.

## The theme: Scale the boundary around ownership

Creating a new environment for each use case can feel safe because every solution is separated. It also creates administrative overhead and makes policy maintenance harder. Related applications and agents often share the same owners, support model, and data boundary.

The group discussed moving toward team-focused environments while balancing security, scalability, and administrative effort. A higher-level approval path was still required before changing the naming and setup standards.

This was an important distinction. We reached a governance direction, but not a final enterprise standard. The proposal still needed formal review and durable documentation.

## Field notes from the build

One environment request had been approved but remained unprovisioned. A security-group request had been submitted, and further action depended on an administrator's return. I treated the delay as an ownership and process signal rather than a technical failure.

The review identified a repeatable administrative requirement: Power Platform administrators should be owners of the relevant security groups and receive system-administrator access as part of environment creation. Adding those permissions after a support incident creates avoidable friction.

Support environments were another design need. The group discussed whether support staff should have one shared sandbox or separate environments across organizational boundaries. The decision was not finalized. The requirement was clear, however: troubleshooting should happen in a controlled support space rather than in a maker's active environment. #type/decision

DLP and connector placement were discussed alongside environment structure. The environment boundary cannot be designed independently from the policy boundary. Too many narrow environments can create duplicated DLP administration, while overly broad environments can mix unrelated connector and data risks.

Naming conventions became part of the same problem. A durable name should communicate stable scope and survive organizational changes where possible. It should not be tied so closely to a single agent or temporary initiative that the environment becomes misleading six months later.

The meeting also called for a Center of Excellence walkthrough to improve shared understanding of available governance capabilities. That walkthrough was assigned, not completed. #type/win

I excluded a separate document-bot invitation because the calendar only showed that I had not responded. An invitation is not evidence of attendance or progress.

## What moved

-

Advanced a team-focused environment model as an alternative to agent-specific sprawl.

-

Defined formal approval as the next gate for naming and governance changes.

-

Established administrator ownership and system access as environment-creation requirements.

-

Identified a controlled support sandbox as a platform need.

-

Connected environment structure with DLP and connector-management strategy.

-

Documented the delayed environment request and its dependency on security-group provisioning.

-

Assigned a platform-governance walkthrough to improve shared operational understanding.

## The reusable pattern

Use the fewest environments that preserve meaningful boundaries.

Group solutions by durable owner, data boundary, support model, and lifecycle. Create a separate environment when a workload has materially different security, connector, compliance, capacity, or operational requirements.

Provision each environment from a standard manifest that includes the security group, administrator roles, naming, DLP policy, allowed connectors, support owner, and lifecycle stage. The process should be idempotent and produce an auditable result.

The future state is policy-driven provisioning with drift detection. The platform can compare deployed configuration with the approved manifest and route exceptions before they become support incidents.

## Standing takeaways

-

Maximum isolation can create minimum operability.

-

Stable ownership is a stronger default boundary than a temporary use case.

-

Environment provisioning should include support access from the beginning.

-

DLP strategy and environment strategy must be designed together.

-

A governance direction is not final until it is approved and documented.

## Open thread

The next step is to formalize the team-focused proposal, resolve the delayed provision request, choose the support-environment model, and turn the baseline into a reusable provisioning manifest.
