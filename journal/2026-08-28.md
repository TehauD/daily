# The Daily — Who Owns It When It Breaks?

Friday. I protect the bookends of this day on purpose — a morning block for stretch/meditation/coffee, a run at lunch, and an hour of weekly reflection before I log off. In between, the week's loudest lesson finally said itself out loud: the hard part of shipping automation isn't building it, it's answering the question *"and who owns this when it breaks at 2 a.m.?"* Everything today circled ownership, accountability, and the quiet discipline of not over-building.

---

## The theme: capability without an owner is a liability

Turning something on is easy. Being accountable for it forever is the actual commitment. Three moments today were the same question wearing different clothes.

## Field notes from the AI-tooling churn
- A supplier's cybersecurity incident rippled straight into our week — blocked domains, unconfirmed orders, rescheduled meetings. A reminder that your risk surface includes everyone you integrate with.
- The path to enabling a risky capability finally got clearer: a small monthly spend cap, a defined owner, and a way to measure value — a POC framing, not a blank check. That's how you say "yes" responsibly.
- Once one team gets a useful automation, everyone wants it. The right response isn't to hand-build it ten times — it's a shared, copyable template library with just enough governance that it doesn't sprawl.

## The reusable-pattern throughline
Same reflex, last day of the week: turn one-offs into shared infrastructure.
- A copyable "starter flows" repository → so the next person clones instead of rebuilds.
- An ownership/backup checklist for any enabled capability → nothing patient- or business-critical rides on a single person.
- A POC framing (cap + owner + metric) → a repeatable way to greenlight risky-but-valuable things.
- A protected weekly reflection block → the one ritual that keeps all of the above honest.

## Standing takeaways
- **Every capability needs an owner and a backup — before you enable it.** "It works" is not the finish line; "someone's accountable when it doesn't" is.
- **Just because you *can* automate it doesn't mean you should.** If it can be over-engineered, it will be — add the guardrail before the group chat does it for laughs.
- **Your resilience is only as strong as your dependencies.** Plan for a partner's bad day, not just your own.
- **Say yes responsibly.** A capped, measured, owned POC beats both a reckless "sure" and a reflexive "no."
- **Protect the reflection.** The hour to step back and plan is the highest-leverage hour of the week — defend it like a deadline.
