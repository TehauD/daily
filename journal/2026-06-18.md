# The Daily: Document Ownership Before Scale

The application inventory was not just a list of apps. It was an attempt to make ownership, support, security, lineage, and deployment visible before the platform grew further.

## The theme: Operational metadata is part of the application

I participated in a transcribed walkthrough of Power Apps lineage documentation. The record captured the application, environment, audience, primary and secondary development owners, operational support, access-management roles, and data sources.

The group identified missing process layers around deployment, training, enhancement intake, and ticket enforcement. I accepted follow-up to help formalize a Power Apps deployment task process and clarify the enhancement route.

I also added a security remediation item to the tracking list. The work was documented and queued, not completed. #type/win

## Field notes from the build

The inventory separated development ownership from operational support. That distinction is essential. The person who can modify a canvas app may not be the person responsible for outages, permissions, or business continuity.

The session also emphasized group-managed access where possible. The intent was to reduce direct support dependency by assigning appropriate stakeholders to manage users. That creates scale only when group owners and escalation paths remain current.

I coordinated a policy correction for Copilot environments. The environments needed to be added to an exclusion list on one data-loss-prevention policy while remaining governed by the Copilot-specific policy. The requested change was completed, and I committed to reflect it in documentation. #type/win

I continued enablement work by supporting an Agent in a Day participant with environment access and import expectations. I also shared an early peer-review agent proof of concept grounded in the existing ML document library. That was a testable prototype, not an approved review authority.

A deployment conversation revisited a prior application upgrade. The safe path included backing up blue and live slots, producing a manifest, confirming custom files, and backing up the database. I explained that earlier automation attempts failed around schema changes and initial configuration, so the working method remained partly manual.

## The reusable pattern

Require an operational manifest for every app: owners, support contacts, audience, environment, data sources, connectors, groups, policies, deployment method, training, monitoring, and retirement path.

## Standing takeaways

-

Ownership and support are different responsibilities.

-

Policy exceptions need documentation and a bounded scope.

-

A prototype can accelerate learning without being treated as an authority.
