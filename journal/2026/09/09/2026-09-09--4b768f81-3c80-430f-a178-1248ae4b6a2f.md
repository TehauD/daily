The Daily: Capability Moves Faster Than Governance

The challenge is no longer determining whether AI can solve a problem. The challenge is selecting the correct level of investment for the problem being solved.

Today I worked through tradeoffs between managed tools, custom applications, local AI, and enterprise platforms while discussing future AI directions with leadership and collaborators. #type/learning

The theme: Avoid both over-engineering and under-engineering

Some requests can now be solved through rapidly emerging platforms that did not exist only months earlier.

That reality changes how architecture decisions should be made.

Field notes from the build

Patient experience intelligence continued to mature as an interim solution designed to provide AI-assisted exploration while preserving a path toward a long-term enterprise architecture.

The conversations reinforced the importance of balancing people, process, and technology together rather than optimizing only the technology dimension. #type/learning

What moved

-Refined patient experience intelligence concepts.

-Framed an AI decision and cost model discussion.

-Evaluated local AI and enterprise AI pathways.

-Expanded stakeholder engagement.

The reusable pattern

Start with the least complex architecture capable of satisfying evidence, governance, and operational requirements.

The future state is a formal decision framework that recommends the correct delivery model for each AI initiative.

Standing takeaways

-Architecture is a business decision.

-Custom development is not always the answer.

-People and process matter as much as technology.

Open thread

Decision thresholds still need clearer definition as platform options continue to evolve.
