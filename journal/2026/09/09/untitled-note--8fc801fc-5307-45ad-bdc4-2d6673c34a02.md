# AI Architecture: Capability Is Moving Faster Than the Decision Framework

The difficult AI question is no longer whether a use case can be built. It is deciding which delivery pattern deserves a notebook, an assistant, an agent, a local model, or a fully engineered application.

Today I worked through that decision pressure across contract analysis, STCA grounding, patient-experience intelligence, and the AI Champions program. The lesson was that a changing platform landscape makes architecture discipline more important, not less. #type/learning

## The theme: Choose the minimum architecture that satisfies the evidence requirement

A leadership discussion surfaced the risk of turning each request into a custom workflow when a managed capability may become the better long-term path. The practical tension was clear: over-engineering wastes scarce capacity, while under-engineering leaves strategic work without controls.

I framed the need for a decision and cost model that distinguishes simple synthesis from durable, high-fidelity systems. Contract analysis raised the bar because the governing standard, source agreement, extraction, findings, and provenance must remain consistent across runs.

## Field notes from the build

I assembled the STCA and contract references needed to test a local comparison pattern. In parallel, I continued shaping a patient-experience workspace around scorecards, trends, an AI advisor, initiatives, and deterministic import and export paths.

The leadership checkpoint did not finalize the platform direction. It did, however, reinforce a useful constraint: capability alone is not sufficient justification for a custom build. #type/learning

## What moved

-Framed a decision problem across managed AI, developer assistants, cloud platforms, local inference, and custom applications.

-Identified repeatability, evidence, governance, cost, and maintenance as architecture-selection criteria.

-Prepared STCA and contract artifacts for a grounded comparison workflow.

-Advanced a patient-experience intelligence workspace with scorecard, trend, initiative, and export surfaces.

-Proposed a local-AI technology and GRC review as a governed exploration path.

## The reusable pattern

Use a tiered architecture rubric: begin with the least complex pattern, then escalate only when evidence, control, integration, scale, or lifecycle requirements demand it.

The future state is an automated intake service that scores each AI request against risk, cost, determinism, data sensitivity, integration, and operating burden before recommending a delivery pattern.

## Standing takeaways

-Just because a workflow can be custom-built does not mean it should be.

-A notebook and a governed application solve different evidence problems.

-Architecture selection needs an explicit cost and control model.

## Open thread

The decision framework still needs agreed thresholds for when a request should move from managed productivity tooling into an engineered product.
