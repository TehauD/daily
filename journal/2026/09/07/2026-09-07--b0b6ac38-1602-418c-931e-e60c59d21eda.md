The Daily: A Review Score Is Not Yet Evidence

A contract-analysis workspace becomes trustworthy only when every finding can be traced back to a governing requirement, supporting clause, and reviewer decision.

Today I expanded ATA from a contract viewer into a structured review workspace focused on STCA alignment, material deviations, evidence support, redlines, and traceability. The interface looked increasingly complete, but the larger lesson was that legal intelligence depends on provenance more than presentation. #type/learning

The theme: Make findings reproducible

The workspace organized contract review around the source agreement, governing requirements, drift analysis, evidence, timelines, and review history.

That shift made an important distinction visible. Alignment percentages and risk classifications are outputs. Evidence is the collection of facts required to reproduce them.

Field notes from the build

I reviewed adjacent DevOps, APIOps, testing, monitoring, and deployment patterns while refining the contract workflow.

Those systems reinforced the same architectural principle: every promoted conclusion needs lineage and operational controls. #type/learning

What moved

-Expanded ATA into a traceable contract-intelligence workspace.

-Separated source content, findings, evidence, drift, and review history.

-Introduced clearer STCA comparison concepts.

-Connected legal review workflows to broader lifecycle-management practices.

The reusable pattern

Treat every finding as a versioned object that stores source evidence, governing requirement, reviewer disposition, and release state.

The future state is a deterministic contract-review pipeline where analyses can be reproduced and audited.

Standing takeaways

-A score without evidence is only a signal.

-The source document must remain primary.

-Traceability is a product feature.

Open thread

The ingestion and review pipeline still needs stronger validation and provenance controls.
