The Daily: A Prototype Is a Conversation Starter

The first version of an application rarely succeeds because of what it automates. It succeeds because it gives stakeholders something concrete to react to.

Today the Patient Experience prototype became less about screens and more about feedback. Early reviews helped surface gaps, assumptions, and opportunities that were difficult to identify from requirements alone. #type/learning

The theme: Build something people can critique

Prototype discussions focused on creating an interim solution that could accelerate learning while preserving a path toward a longer-term enterprise platform.

The most valuable output was not agreement. It was better questions.

Field notes from the build

Feedback loops exposed opportunities around intake workflows, AI-assisted exploration, traceability, and operational fit.

The prototype shifted from being a technical artifact into a collaboration artifact. #type/learning

What moved
-Validated early Patient Experience concepts.
-Expanded stakeholder feedback.
-Identified workflow and intake gaps.
-Refined future-state architecture thinking.

The reusable pattern

Deliver prototypes early enough that stakeholders can change direction before significant investment occurs.

The future state is continuous discovery informed by working software.

Standing takeaways
-Feedback is a feature.
-Prototypes reduce ambiguity.
-Early validation reduces rework.

Open thread

The long-term data lineage and governance model still requires additional definition.
