# ⚗ Experiment

## The Daily: Make the Equation Something You Can Interrogate

Mathematics becomes easier to understand when its structure can be seen, changed, and compared.

Today I explored an interactive laboratory for studying fluid behavior, conservation laws, and the boundary between stable numerical solutions and unresolved mathematical questions.

The objective was not to claim a solution to a famous problem. It was to create an environment where assumptions could be inspected and their consequences observed.

## The theme: Perturb the model to understand the model

A static equation communicates formal structure. An interactive model can reveal sensitivity.

I wanted the laboratory to make it possible to adjust terms, initial conditions, boundary assumptions, forcing, viscosity, compressibility, gravity, and numerical resolution. The goal was to expose what each component contributes rather than treating the equation as an indivisible object.

That distinction matters in both education and research.

## Field notes from the build

Visualization is useful only when it remains connected to the underlying mathematics.

A compelling animation can conceal numerical instability just as easily as it can reveal a pattern. The laboratory therefore needs to distinguish:

- Physical behavior
- Numerical approximation
- Model assumptions
- Solver limitations
- Visualization artifacts

Compression also changed the conceptual boundary. An incompressible formulation cannot simply be presented as though it represents every fluid regime.

## What moved

- Expanded an interactive fluid-dynamics laboratory.
- Added configurable equation terms and environmental variables.
- Incorporated compressible and incompressible modeling considerations.
- Explored time-lapse and perturbation-based analysis.
- Considered localized AI assistance for explanation and experiment management. #type/experiment
- Strengthened the distinction between mathematical evidence and visual intuition.

## The reusable pattern

Expose assumptions as controls, but preserve mathematically valid configurations as named presets.

This allows exploration without implying that every possible combination represents a coherent physical model.

## Future state

An AI lab assistant could explain each parameter, detect unstable configurations, compare runs, and generate an evidence-linked experiment journal. #type/experiment

The model would support learning and analysis without presenting simulation output as proof.

## Standing takeaways

- Interactivity can reveal assumptions hidden by notation.
- A simulation is an experiment within a model, not reality itself. #type/experiment
- Numerical stability and mathematical truth are different questions.
- AI can help explain a laboratory without becoming the authority over it.

## Open thread

The next challenge is building an explanation layer that connects every visual change to the exact term, assumption, or numerical method responsible for it.
