# The Daily: Governance Needs a Support Model

The platform could enable more builders, but neither side of the organization had resources committed to support citizen-developed AI projects. That is not a minor operating detail. It is a boundary on what can responsibly scale.

Today separated enthusiasm for low-code AI from the support obligations that follow it.

## The theme: Do not launch demand without a recovery path

I participated in a transcribed Power Platform baseline review. The discussion covered Microsoft feature changes, Copilot credit usage, departmental environment naming, an application lifecycle management workshop, and the lack of support capacity for citizen AI development.

A concrete action was to bring the support gap to governance. That is the right venue because the issue cannot be solved through better prompts or another environment. If teams are encouraged to build critical workflows, someone must own incidents, platform changes, access problems, cost controls, and retirement.

The group also agreed to monitor production credit consumption before advising teams to purchase capacity. That preserved evidence over speculation. Departmental affiliation also mattered because the environment name and credit scope depended on it.

## Field notes from the build

The meeting planned an application lifecycle management workshop once key people were available. The need was clear: developers require a shared path for source control, environment promotion, configuration, and support. The workshop was scheduled as future work, not completed work. #type/win

A separate enterprise-browser demonstration explored moving some users away from virtual desktops or toward a hybrid model. The transcribed session covered test-user deployment, application analysis, and certificate-based trust for a clinical application. I attended the demonstration, but I did not own or complete those actions. The value of the session was architectural context about endpoint control and application compatibility.

I also joined a short working session related to extracting Google Search Console data with a service account. The invite states that setup was the purpose, and attendance is supported, but no transcript confirms completion. I am recording it as an explored integration path rather than a configured service.

Automated pipeline notices continued to show row-count mismatches and a failed terminology load. They remain supporting evidence only. Their relevance is the support question: someone must interpret warning states, route ownership, and decide when an apparently completed service is not healthy. #type/win

## What moved

-Contributed to a transcribed baseline review of Power Platform governance and support constraints.

-Advanced governance escalation for the lack of citizen-development support capacity.

-Advanced production usage monitoring before Copilot credit purchase recommendations.

-Clarified that departmental affiliation should drive environment naming and cost scope.

-Planned a future application lifecycle management workshop.

-Explored enterprise-browser and service-account integration patterns without overstating implementation.

## The reusable pattern

Every citizen-development program needs a published responsibility matrix covering builder support, platform operations, security review, data ownership, incident response, cost management, and retirement. Projects without an accountable product owner should remain prototypes.

The future-state platform should automate environment assignment, license checks, usage telemetry, policy validation, and release evidence while routing exceptions to named support owners.

## Standing takeaways

-Enablement without support creates hidden operational debt.

-Cost guidance should follow measured usage.

-An environment boundary should reflect ownership and funding.

## Open thread

The unresolved decision is who will support successful citizen-developed AI solutions after they move beyond a bounded pilot.
