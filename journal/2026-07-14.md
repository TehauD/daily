# The Daily: Measure Outcomes, Not Activity

The organization did not need another count of clicks. It needed evidence that AI training, agents, and governance were changing work in a useful and responsible way.

Today connected three parts of that problem: executive training, outcome measurement, and compliance evidence. The throughline was simple. Adoption metrics can show that a tool was opened. They cannot show that the work improved.

## The theme: Build the evidence loop with the program

I attended a transcribed leadership checkpoint for an AI enablement program. The group reviewed executive training and peer mentorship, discussed accelerating delivery of existing material, and considered office hours as an interactive support model. A custom survey was proposed to measure Copilot outcomes beyond activity metrics.

The meeting also assigned me a concrete governance action: engage the appropriate compliance contact and provide documentation for an AI safety attestation. I followed up the same day with relevant internal resources and asked for the review to be completed without overwhelming the recipient with unnecessary detail. #type/win

This was a useful shift from broad AI advocacy to operational proof. Training content, support mechanisms, compliance evidence, and business outcomes need to be designed as one system. Otherwise, the program can accumulate participation without learning whether it is safe or valuable.

## Field notes from the build

The training discussion emphasized using existing material quickly, reviewing it internally, and creating a repeatable handoff to the learning function. That is better than rebuilding every training request from scratch. The remaining design question is how to preserve technical accuracy while making the material concise enough for executives.

The proposed survey matters because activity is an incomplete proxy. A useful measurement model should ask whether the user completed a task faster, improved quality, reduced rework, made a better decision, or discovered a new risk. It should also distinguish self-reported value from observed workflow evidence. #type/win

I also joined a legal AI touch point. The record confirms attendance but does not provide a transcript or substantive outcome. I am treating it as continued development context rather than claiming that the contract assistant advanced to a specific release stage.

Data operations remained noisy in the background. Automated alerts showed row-count mismatches and failed loads. I did not treat receipt of those notifications as completed work. Their value here is editorial: measurement systems need to distinguish a completed run from a trustworthy result. #type/win

## The reusable pattern

Design enablement as an evidence pipeline. Capture the learner, use case, intended outcome, baseline, intervention, observed result, risk signal, and follow-up action. Keep the schema small enough to operate but structured enough to compare patterns.

The future state is continuous intelligence for AI adoption. Training requests trigger an approved content package, users receive bounded support, outcome signals are collected, and recurring failures feed the next training and governance revision.

## Standing takeaways

-

Usage is not outcome.

-

Training, support, measurement, and compliance belong in one operating loop.

-

A completed process still needs evidence that the result is correct. #type/win

## Open thread

The measurement model still needs a defensible balance between lightweight surveys and direct workflow evidence.
